## Reading this honestly

- **CoLA scores 0.2951 AUC — below the 0.5 chance line.** It is not merely failing to find the injected users; it is systematically ranking them as *more* trustworthy than genuine users. This was checked against the raw anomaly score with the trust transform bypassed, and the direction is the same, so it is a property of the detector on this data and not a sign error in the trust mapping.

- **A plausible mechanism.** CoLA's contrastive objective scores a node by how well its own attributes can be predicted from its neighbourhood context. A coordinated campaign is *internally consistent by construction*: the injected users share templates, share a forced rating, and overlap on target items, so each one is unusually easy to predict from the others. A detector that equates 'anomalous' with 'unpredictable from context' will therefore rate a large, well-organised campaign as exemplary. Isolated eccentric users look stranger to it than a botnet does.

- **Why this matters for the recommender.** If trust is anti-correlated with manipulation, any use of it (weighting a loss, weighting an edge) up-weights the manipulated interactions and down-weights genuine ones. The HGT robustness study (`scripts/run_robustness.py`) measures that harm directly.

- **What would have to change.** Either the projection (a user-user co-occurrence graph rewards dense coordinated cliques), or the detector (a density- or reconstruction-based objective rather than a contrastive predictability one), or the target (score the *interaction* rather than the user). The behavioural detector in the table above changes the signal instead: it asks whether an account behaves like a campaign account, which does not reward internal consistency.
- Chance-level AUC is 0.5. Precision@100 should be read against a prevalence of 0.0476.
- The real corpus has no labels, so none of this transfers automatically. The honest claim is about this generative process only.
